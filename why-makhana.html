Why Makhana?

For centuries, makhana has been one of India's best-kept nutritional secrets — a crunchy, protein-rich superfood eaten during fasts, festivals, and everyday snacking. Now, the world is catching on. Here's why this humble fox nut deserves a spot in your pantry.

What is Makhana?

The ancient Indian superfood that's finally getting the recognition it deserves.

Makhana — also known as fox nuts, lotus seeds, or phool makhana — is the edible seed of the Euryale ferox plant, a water lily native to the wetlands of eastern Asia. In India, makhana has been cultivated for thousands of years, deeply woven into Ayurvedic tradition and everyday cooking.

The journey from pond to pouch is fascinatingly hands-on. Makhana is harvested from shallow ponds and marshlands, primarily in Bihar's Mithila region — an area so renowned for its makhana that it holds a GI (Geographical Indication) tag, recognizing the region's unique cultivation expertise and the quality of its produce.

After harvesting, the seeds are sun-dried and then dry-roasted, causing them to pop into the light, crunchy puffs we know and love. No oil, no chemicals — just heat and patience. The result is a naturally low-fat, high-fibre snack that's been a staple during Indian fasting traditions like Navratri, Ekadashi, and Maha Shivaratri for generations.

What makes makhana special isn't just tradition — it's the nutritional profile. Rich in protein, calcium, magnesium, and fibre, and naturally low in fat, makhana stands out as one of the most nutrient-dense snacks available. No wonder it's gone from a fasting food to a fitness food.

Quick Facts

Also known as: Fox nuts, lotus seeds, phool makhana, gorgon nut

Origin: Bihar, India — Mithila region (GI-tagged)

Harvest: Grown in ponds, hand-harvested, sun-dried, then dry-roasted

Tradition: Fasting staple during Navratri, Ekadashi, Maha Shivaratri, and other occasions

Naturally: Gluten-free, plant-based, low-fat, and high in fibre

8 Reasons to Snack on Makhana

From protein to antioxidants — here's what makes makhana a standout snack.

01

Naturally Nutritious

Packed with protein, calcium, and magnesium, makhana delivers real nutrition in every crunchy bite — not empty calories.

02

May Support Weight Management

Low in calories and high in fibre, makhana may help you feel fuller for longer. A 30g handful is just ~105 kcal — a smart swap for heavier snacks.

03

Heart Friendly

With virtually no fat, low sodium, and naturally occurring antioxidants, makhana is traditionally associated with heart-healthy eating patterns.

04

Blood Sugar Friendly

Makhana has a low glycemic index, and its high fibre content may help slow the absorption of sugar into the bloodstream after meals.

05

Rich in Antioxidants

Makhana contains gallic acid and other polyphenols — antioxidants that may help protect cells and are traditionally associated with long-term wellness.

06

Plant-Based Protein

At 9.7g protein per 100g, makhana is a solid plant-based protein source — great for vegetarians, vegans, and anyone looking to snack smarter.

07

Naturally Gluten Free

Makhana is inherently gluten-free, making it a safe, worry-free snack for those with celiac disease or gluten sensitivity.

08

No Preservatives Needed

Once roasted, makhana is naturally shelf-stable. No artificial preservatives, no chemical treatments — just pure, clean snacking the way nature intended.
